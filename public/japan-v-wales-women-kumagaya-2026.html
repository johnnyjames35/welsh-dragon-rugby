Japan 5-29 Wales: Five Tries Seal the Series in Kumagaya

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 31 October 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Wales Women finished their 2026 international year in the best possible way, beating Japan 29-5 at Kumagaya Rugby Stadium to win the two-Test WXV Global Series tour 2-0.

Sean Lynn's side ran in five tries and led 19-5 at half-time. Across the two Tests Wales scored 46 points and conceded just five, and it is their first pair of back-to-back wins since 2024.

Half-time: Japan 5-19 Wales. Venue: Kumagaya Rugby Stadium. Competition: WXV Global Series, second Test.

A fast start

The weather was far kinder than in Tokyo, and Wales played with tempo from the first whistle. A kick-pass from Lleucu George and a chip ahead from Nel Metcalfe took them into the Japanese 22. A big carry from co-captain Alex Callender kept the pressure on, and the forwards went close before the ball was swung out wide. Metcalfe ran in to open the scoring after seven minutes, and George, who started at fly-half in place of Keira Bevan, added the conversion.

Wales then paid for not clearing their lines. Japan hooker Moe Nagaoka scored from a driving lineout on 14 minutes, and that was the only score the hosts managed all day. Wales answered with a lineout drive of their own, and hooker Kelsey Jones scored on 24 minutes with another George conversion.

Seren Lockwood then sniped round the short side of a maul near the Japanese 22 to send Amy Williams over on 38 minutes. George hit the upright with the conversion, but Wales went in at 19-5.

Two more tries after the break

Japan flanker Jennifer Nduka was sent to the sin-bin five minutes into the second half for punching the ball out of Lockwood's hands at a breakdown. Wales made the extra player count straight away, with prop Sisilia Tuipulotu powering over from close range. A long cut-out pass from Callender then created the space for Williams to score her second try, and the lead was 29-5. George hit the post with the conversion attempt on Tuipulotu's try and missed the one on Williams' second.

Down to 13, but Wales hold on

Wales looked for a sixth try. Williams forced a five-metre scrum with a kick and chase, but Japan ripped the ball away from Keira Bevan and cleared their lines.

The final quarter then got harder. Bevan, on as a replacement scrum-half, was shown a yellow card for a deliberate knock-on. Shortly afterwards a late call-up to the bench was also sent to the sin-bin for pulling an opponent's hair in the tackle, which left Wales with 13 players. They defended well, with a Callender turnover on her own line denying Japan a second try, and George kicked the ball out to finish the game.

What it means for Wales

After the Tokyo win, Lynn said his team had to be more accurate and produce a consistent 80 minutes. Five tries and a 24-point winning margin, with Courtney Keight starting at inside centre in place of the injured Freya Bell, was a strong answer.

It means Wales finish their WXV Global Series campaign with three wins from four:

Wales v Japan: The Full Head-to-Head Record Before 7 November

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 29 October 2026 | ⏱ 5 min read

For most of the professional era, Wales against Japan was one of the safer fixtures on the calendar. In 2025 that stopped being true. Japan won in Kitakyushu in July, Wales needed a last-kick penalty in Cardiff in November, and the old assumptions are gone.

The two sides meet again on Saturday 7 November 2026 at the Principality Stadium, the opening home match of Wales' November Nations Championship games. Here is the full record, the 2025 matches in detail, and what to expect.

Wales have never lost to Japan in Cardiff, winning all six meetings there.

Japan have won twice, and both times in Japan. The first was 23-8 in Tokyo in June 2013, a week after Wales won the first Test in Osaka. The second was in Kitakyushu in July 2025.

The big scores, and the close ones

The early history was one-sided. Wales won 98-0 at the Millennium Stadium in November 2004, still their biggest ever Test win, and scored more than 50 in six others. Japan's best performance before 2025 was the 2016 Cardiff Test, when they scored three tries and 30 points before Wales won 33-30 with a drop goal from Sam Davies in front of nearly 74,000.

The last four meetings have all been decided by nine points or fewer: 3 (2016), 5, 9 and 1 (all in 2025).

2025: the year it changed

Wales' mid-year tour of Japan came at the end of a long run of defeats. In Kitakyushu on 5 July, Japan won 24-19, their first win over a tier-one nation since beating Scotland at the 2019 World Cup, and Wales fell to 14th in the world rankings, their lowest since the rankings began in 2003.

A week later in Kobe, Wales won 31-22 to end a losing run widely reported as 18 Tests, their first win since beating Georgia at the 2023 World Cup.

Then came Cardiff on 15 November 2025. Wales led 7-0 after Dan Edwards' early try, but Japan had levelled by the quarter-hour. The game turned on discipline: Japan were down to 13 players for a spell after a sin-bin and a yellow card, but when Josh Adams' yellow was upgraded to a 20-minute red, Wales failed to score while Japan had the extra men. Louis Rees-Zammit and Nick Tompkins scored Wales' other tries, Japan went 23-21 ahead through a penalty, and Jarrod Evans kicked the winner with the last kick of the match.

That win ended a run of 10 successive home defeats for Wales, dating back to 2023. Steve Tandy admitted afterwards that he was relieved rather than happy.

The match: 7 November 2026

It is round four of the Nations Championship, and the first of three home matches in a row, followed by New Zealand on 14 November and Australia on 21 November.

Squad news

Tandy named a 39-man squad on 20 October (17 backs, 22 forwards), with three uncapped players: Aneurin Owen of the Dragons, Che Hope and Rhys Barratt. Hope has been called up because Ospreys scrum-half Kieran Hardy faces a lengthy lay-off with a knee injury. The big omission is Taulupe Faletau, who has only just returned from injury and is not in the group, although Tandy has not ruled him out longer term. Leicester back-rows Tommy Reffell and Olly Cracknell were also left out.

The match-day 23 will be named in the build-up, and we will update this page when it is. For the full group, see our Wales Nations Championship squad page.

What to watch

  • Discipline. Last November's match was decided by cards. A repeat of that against a side as quick as Japan is dangerous.
  • Japan's attack. Japan scored three tries in Cardiff in 2016 and two in 2025, and they have scored 22 or more in each of the last four meetings.
  • Home form. The win over Japan last November only ended a run of 10 successive home defeats, so Wales will want to show that was a turning point.
  • The week after. New Zealand are in Cardiff seven days later, so selection and rotation will be watched closely. Our Wales v New Zealand head-to-head covers that game.

On the record, Wales are heavy favourites. On the last three results, nobody should be taking this one for granted.

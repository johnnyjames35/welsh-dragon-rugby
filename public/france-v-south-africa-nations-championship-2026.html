France 21-19 South Africa: Dupont Runs the Show as Les Bleus Hold Off the Springboks in Paris

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 13 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

France beat South Africa 21-19 at the Stade de France on Friday night, in Round 5 of the Nations Championship. It was their first win over the Springboks in Paris since 2005. They led 21-7 at half-time after three tries, then held on as the world champions scored twice more in the second half and finished two points short. Antoine Dupont was named Man of the Match, playing at fly-half because Matthieu Jalibert, Romain Ntamack and Thomas Ramos were all injured.

Who scored

TeamScorers
France (21)Tries: Aaron Grandidier-Nkanang, Théo Attissogbe, Louis Bielle-Biarrey. Conversions: Maxime Lucu (3)
South Africa (19)Tries: Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Cobus Reinach, Malcolm Marx. Conversions: Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu (2)

Half-time: France 21-7 South Africa. The reports we checked differ by a minute or so on some tries, so we have kept the timings loose.

First half: France fast out of the blocks

South Africa lock Lood de Jager was shown a yellow card early on for a high tackle on Lucu, and France made the most of it. A break from Kalvin Gourgues set up Lucu to find Grandidier-Nkanang out on the right, and the wing dived over around the quarter-hour mark.

The second try came from a Dupont 50/22. From the lineout that followed, a pass from Yoram Moefana bounced off Gourgues’ head and Attissogbe gathered it in the in-goal area to make it 12-0.

The Springboks answered the way they usually do, with a scrum penalty, lineout pressure and a rolling maul that finished with Wessels over the line. France replied almost straight away. Dupont put a kick in behind the defence, Bielle-Biarrey beat Kurt-Lee Arendse to it, and Lucu landed a difficult conversion for 21-7.

Rassie Erasmus then changed his entire front row after about 35 minutes. Arendse had a try ruled out just before the break because he was not far enough back from the lineout, and Lucu was shown a yellow card for a high tackle before half-time. Over the match France had three yellow cards (Lucu, Grandidier-Nkanang and Montagne), while South Africa had one yellow (De Jager) and one red (Wiese).

Second half: Springboks surge, France hold

South Africa had Jasper Wiese sent off early in the second half for a dangerous clear-out, yet they were the team on top for much of the half. When Grandidier-Nkanang was yellow-carded for a deliberate knock-on, Reinach scored close to the posts straight afterwards and it was 21-14.

France then lost Régis Montagne to a yellow card for collapsing a scrum, and Lucu missed a kick at goal with about ten minutes left that could have put them two scores clear. Marx went over for South Africa after the 70th minute, but the conversion was missed, leaving it 21-19.

There was one more scare. A knock-on near halfway with around two minutes left gave the Boks a chance, but France did not concede a penalty. Bielle-Biarrey then made a big tackle on Jesse Kriel to force the turnover, and Lucu kicked the ball into the stands to end it.

What it means

The win keeps France top of the northern hemisphere table, and it was South Africa’s first defeat of this Nations Championship. Before the game the Springboks and New Zealand were level on points at the top of the southern table, and the NZ Herald reported that the result leaves the All Blacks with their destiny in their own hands. The finals weekend is at Twickenham from 27 to 29 November.

It also reverses recent history. South Africa won the last meeting 32-17 in Paris in November 2025 and the 2023 World Cup quarter-final 29-28. Wales are next up against New Zealand on Saturday: see our Wales v New Zealand head-to-head.

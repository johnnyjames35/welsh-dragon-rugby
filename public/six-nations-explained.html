The Six Nations Explained: Format, Bonus Points and Trophies

✎ By John James | 📅 20 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

The Six Nations is the annual men’s rugby union championship between England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland and Wales. The 2027 championship runs from Friday 5 February to Saturday 13 March. This guide explains how it works, how the table is decided and which trophies are at stake, with a Wales focus.

The format

Each of the six teams plays the other five once, so there are five rounds and 15 matches. A team plays two or three home games a year, and home advantage swaps from one year to the next. The tournament has been played by six nations since Italy joined in 2000. In 2027 there is one fallow week, after Round 3, and the final round is a ‘Super Saturday’ with all three matches played on 13 March. See Wales’ 2027 fixtures.

How the table works

The Six Nations has used bonus points since 2017.

ResultPoints
Win4
Draw2
Scoring four or more tries in a match1 bonus point
Losing by seven points or fewer1 bonus point
Winning all five matches (Grand Slam)3 extra points

The Grand Slam bonus exists so that a team that wins all five matches can never be overtaken by a team on bonus points. Ireland were the first to win those three extra points, in 2018, and Wales did it in 2019. A Grand Slam winner therefore finishes with at least 23 points. In 2026 France won the title on 21 points without a Grand Slam, with Ireland second on 19.

Grand Slam and Triple Crown

A Grand Slam is won by any team that wins all five matches. The Triple Crown can only be won by one of the four home nations, England, Ireland, Scotland or Wales, by beating the other three. A physical Triple Crown trophy has been awarded since 2006, and it is often a consolation prize for a team that misses out on the title. In 2027 Wales play Scotland, Ireland and England, so the Triple Crown is available to them.

The rivalry trophies

As well as the championship trophy, several matches carry their own trophy, which is awarded to the winner of that fixture.

TrophyContested by
Calcutta CupEngland v Scotland (since 1879)

Centenary QuaichIreland v Scotland
Giuseppe Garibaldi TrophyFrance v Italy (introduced 2007)
Auld Alliance TrophyFrance v Scotland
Cuttitta CupItaly v Scotland
Doddie Weir CupScotland v Wales

The Calcutta Cup is the oldest and was contested before the championship began. Wales’ own trophy is the Doddie Weir Cup, played for against Scotland. In 2027 that match is in Round 2, at Murrayfield on 13 February.

How to follow the tournament

The BBC and ITV share the live coverage free-to-air in the UK, with S4C showing Wales matches in Welsh. Our Wales fixtures and how-to-watch page has the dates and kick-off times, and the Women’s Six Nations guide covers the women’s tournament, which starts on 10 April.

Quick answers

How many points do you get for a win in the Six Nations?

A win is worth four points and a draw two. There is a bonus point for scoring four or more tries and another for losing by seven points or fewer.

What is a Grand Slam in the Six Nations?

A Grand Slam is won by a team that wins all five of its matches. It also earns three extra points, so the team cannot be overtaken on bonus points.

What is the Triple Crown?

The Triple Crown can only be won by England, Ireland, Scotland or Wales, by beating the other three home nations in the same championship.

What is the Doddie Weir Cup?

The Doddie Weir Cup is the trophy played for by Scotland and Wales in the Six Nations.

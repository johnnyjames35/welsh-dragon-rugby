Dragons 29-14 Perpignan: Four Tries, a Comeback and No Bonus Point

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 17 October 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Dragons opened their 2026-27 Challenge Cup campaign with a 29-14 win over French side Perpignan at Rodney Parade on Friday night. Filo Tiatia's men were behind at half-time but scored three second-half tries to take control.

It was not their most polished night. But it was a win against Top 14 opposition, and it came on a big night for one of their own: wing Rio Dyer made his 100th appearance for the club.

How the game turned

A fast start, then a flat first half

Dragons were quick out of the blocks. In the fifth minute, wing Jared Rosser went over down the left after a long attacking spell. That try took him to 37 for the club, level with Ashton Hewitt in second place on the Dragons' all-time list, six behind Aled Brew.

After that, Perpignan took over. They had most of the ball and most of the territory, and flanker Jules Coulon scored from close range just before the half-hour. Full-back Tristan Tedder converted, and the French side went in 7-5 up. Dragons had a try from Jac Lloyd ruled out for crossing.

Three second-half tries settle it

The breakthrough came after 55 minutes. Quick ball from a line-out gave centre Aneurin Owen a chance, and he went through some weak defence to score. Perpignan hit back when lock Posolo Tuilagi crashed over, putting the visitors back in front.

Then fly-half Tinus de Beer kicked a penalty to make it 15-14 with a quarter of an hour left. From there, Dragons finished strongly. Tonga wing Anzelo Tuitavuki, on as a replacement, scored from close range, and De Beer added a tricky conversion. Late on, replacement centre Fetuli Paea finished a superb move for his first Dragons try.

Why there was no bonus point

Four tries would have earned a bonus point in past seasons. Not any more.

New bonus-point rule. In this season's Challenge Cup, a team only gets a try bonus point if it scores at least three more tries than the opposition. Scoring four tries is no longer enough on its own.

Perpignan scored two tries, so Dragons needed five. They got four. It still leaves them in a strong position, with two of their next three pool games in Wales.

Rio Dyer reaches 100

The homegrown wing marked his 100th Dragons appearance with a start, and was involved in the move for the final try. It is a big milestone for a player who has come through the Gwent system and gone on to play for Wales.

The cost of the win

The result was not cheap. Loose-head props Rhodri Jones and Wyn Jones both went off injured, a week after Argentina international Rodrigo Martinez was hurt in the defeat to Ospreys. With several key players already rested or injured, Tiatia's front-row depth will be tested.

What comes next for Dragons

The Challenge Cup takes a break while the URC returns, and next up is a Welsh derby away to Ospreys. In Europe, Dragons face South Africa's Cheetahs in December at the redeveloped St Helen's in Swansea, then Bayonne in Newport and Ulster in Belfast in January.

Last season Dragons reached the Challenge Cup semi-finals. A home win to start, even without the bonus point, is a solid first step towards another run.

Wales 35-28 New Zealand: The Full Story of the Day Wales Ended 73 Years of Waiting

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 16 November 2026 | ⏱ 14 min read

Some results are bigger than the game they come from. On Saturday 14 November 2026, in front of 55,152 people at the Principality Stadium, Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 for the first time since 19 December 1953. Thirty-four defeats in a row, spread across nearly three-quarters of a century, ended in one afternoon in Cardiff.

This is the long version. Our match report has the quick facts; here we go through the history that made the wait so heavy, how the game unfolded, the defensive stand that won it, what the people involved said, and what it does and does not tell us about this Wales team.

1. The weight of 1953

To understand why Cardiff reacted as it did, start with the record. Wales and New Zealand had met 38 times before this game, and New Zealand had won 35 of them. Wales' three wins came in 1905, 1935 and 1953, all in Cardiff, and the last of them, 13-8 at the National Stadium on 19 December 1953, was so long ago that it belongs to a different era of the sport.

The first, in 1905, is one of the most famous results in All Blacks history: Wales won 3-0 and New Zealand's Bob Deans claimed a try that was never awarded. The second, in 1935, was 13-12. After 1953 the run began, and the closest the All Blacks came to losing in Cardiff were a 26-25 win in November 2004 and a 13-12 win in 1978. The full list is in our head-to-head record.

The recent past was harder still. New Zealand scored more than 50 points on each of their three previous visits to Cardiff, including 54-16 in 2021, 55-23 in 2022 and 52-26 a year ago. Even that last game, which Wales lost by 26 points, had been called a step forward, because Tom Rogers scored a hat-trick and Wales crossed for four tries. It was the first time Wales had scored four tries against the All Blacks since the 2003 World Cup. The question was never whether Wales could score; it was whether they could ever stop the other side doing the same.

Captain Dewi Lake put the scale of it in perspective before kick-off, saying of the losing run: “I don’t think that I was alive for half of them.” That is the sense in which this was a generational result. Almost nobody in the Welsh squad, and a great many in the crowd, had never seen their country beat New Zealand.

2. The build-up

Wales came into the game after a 28-21 bonus-point win over Japan the weekend before, a result that head coach Steve Tandy said had to be improved on. By the numbers, that was a modest platform. Wales had won two of their first four Nations Championship games and were still fifth in the northern hemisphere table. Nothing about the build-up suggested history.

New Zealand arrived unbeaten in the competition, with four wins from four, and head coach Dave Rennie made eight changes from the side that played Scotland. ESPN’s report suggested those changes backfired. That is a reasonable reading of the result, but it should be held lightly: New Zealand still scored four tries and finished within seven points, and rotation does not explain a 346-tackle defensive performance.

Wales named their team earlier in the week; see our team announcement piece for the line-up. The one point worth stressing is what Tandy asked of the players. According to Swansea Bay News, he told them to focus on their own game rather than New Zealand’s reputation. That is a standard coaching line, but few Welsh sides have managed to live by it for a full 80 minutes against this opponent.

3. First half: Wales race away, the All Blacks hit back

The story of the afternoon started in the second minute. Wales turned the All Blacks over outside their own 22, Louis Rees-Zammit chipped ahead down the right, and Tomos Williams won the race to the bouncing ball and scored. Sam Costelow converted, and it was 7-0 before the stadium had settled.

Four minutes later it was 14-0. Dewi Lake went over from close range, and Costelow added the extras again. It was the first of three occasions on which Wales led by 14 points.

New Zealand were not going to fold. Around the tenth minute Cortez Ratima scored after a lineout, and Richie Mo’unga converted. Wales answered inside 25 minutes: Archie Griffin scored from a lineout, Costelow converted, and Wales led 21-7.

Then came the passage that makes this game so interesting. Wallace Sititi scored around the half-hour mark to make it 21-14. Late in the half Wales had the chance to kick the ball out and head to the interval with a lead. According to Sky Sports, they kept playing instead, and the All Blacks scored in dead time through Fehi Fineanganofo. Mo’unga converted and it was 21-21 at half-time.

A 14-point lead had gone, and the cheap reading is that Wales threw it away. A fairer one is that the decision to play on shows a team willing to attack rather than manage the clock, even against New Zealand, even when 21-14 up. It cost them seven points that time. The second half suggests it also told them something about themselves.

4. Second half: two tries and a lead of 14

If the first half ended on a body blow, the second began with an answer. Within about four minutes of the restart, Eddie James finished in the corner from a Tomos Williams pass, Costelow converted, and it was 28-21.

Then came the decision many people will remember. Wales were awarded a kickable penalty and turned it down, choosing a five-metre lineout instead. They scored from it: Louis Rees-Zammit, in the 48th minute according to Sky Sports, was unopposed. Costelow converted again. Wales led 35-21, for the third time by 14 points, and Costelow had five conversions from five.

New Zealand came straight back. In the 53rd minute Peter Lakai scored after a penalty, Mo’unga converted his fourth conversion of the afternoon, and it was 35-28. That was the last score of the match. Nobody scored a point in the final 27 minutes.

5. The last 27 minutes

This is where the match was won, and it is worth slowing down.

From the moment Lakai scored, the All Blacks did what they do: they held the ball, carried hard, and camped in the Welsh 22. They needed one converted try to level the scores. Over the closing quarter they were held up over the Welsh line three times. Several moments stand out in the reports:

  • Gareth Thomas held up Wallace Sititi over the line around the hour mark.
  • Jac Morgan made a turnover at the breakdown to end an attack that had run through 21 All Blacks phases, according to Sky Sports.
  • James Botham got underneath hooker Asafo Aumua to stop a grounding when it looked certain that New Zealand would score, with about 15 minutes left.
  • Taine Plumtree won two turnovers after coming off the bench, according to Swansea Bay News.
  • With 16 minutes left, Reuben Morgan-Williams and Dan Edwards came on to bring fresh legs to the closing stages.
  • Eddie James also made a try-saving tackle on Anton Lienert-Brown earlier in the game, per Sky.

The totals tell the same story. Wales made 346 tackles across the match, and Dafydd Jenkins, Jac Morgan and James Botham each made more than 30, as reported by Nation.Cymru. Welsh attack scored five tries, which was a first against the All Blacks, but the defence is what ended the run.

This is also the part with the most emotion. Lake revealed afterwards that he stopped watching with 1 minute 20 seconds left and waited for the whistle. When it came, players wept on the pitch, and Botham was reduced to tears in front of the cameras.

6. The people who won it

James Botham, player of the match

The flanker was named player of the match, with a day’s work that included the defining tackle on Aumua and more than 30 tackles.

Dewi Lake, captain

Lake scored the second try, led the side through the half-time collapse and the closing siege, and was clearly moved afterwards. Having lost a 14-point lead once, Wales did not let it happen again.

Sam Costelow, five from five

Costelow converted every try. That sounds routine, but over 80 minutes, with the margin never more than 14, it meant Wales always scored seven for each try rather than five. It is easy to overlook, but those five conversions account for ten points of a seven-point winning margin.

Tomos Williams, Louis Rees-Zammit, Archie Griffin and Eddie James

Williams scored the opener and set up James for the fourth try. Rees-Zammit created the first try with his kick and scored the fifth; see our Louis Rees-Zammit profile. Griffin scored from a lineout. James scored in the corner and made a crucial first-half tackle.

The defensive group

Jenkins, Jac Morgan, Botham and Thomas, along with Plumtree off the bench, make up the group that held out the final quarter. A 346-tackle performance is not the work of three or four individuals; it is the work of a whole team who did not tire.

7. What they said

Steve Tandy said afterwards that “The players were phenomenal.” He also described the result as a real statement win and said he felt pride for the players and for what it meant to the supporters, then pointed straight to Australia the following week.

Lake said it was hard to put into words what the team had done in making history. Botham told ITV the result had come from belief, and credited the crowd and the defensive work the squad had put in.

From the other side, New Zealand captain Beauden Barrett, winning his 149th cap, said that Wales had been like a brick wall at times, and that the All Blacks had built pressure without turning it into points. Rennie praised “a hell of a defensive effort” and said Wales had been clinical when their chances came.

8. What it means, and what it does not

What it means. It is Wales’ first win over New Zealand in 73 years, only their fourth in 39 meetings, and the first time they have scored five tries against them. According to Swansea Bay News it is Steve Tandy’s fifth win in 14 Tests in charge, and it came with a bonus point. For a country that went through a long losing run in recent years, it is a landmark.

In the table, New Zealand picked up two bonus points and moved above South Africa in the Nations Championship table, so the result does not end their hopes of reaching the competition final at Twickenham. For Wales, the immediate task is Australia.

What it does not mean. One game does not prove a rebuilt team. Wales were beaten 0-73 by South Africa in November 2025, albeit without their England and France-based players, and 0-43 by them in July. New Zealand changed eight players. Wales needed to defend for 27 minutes to hold a seven-point lead. Those are all reasons to be careful about how much to read into it.

But there is also a fair answer to that. The ability to score five tries against this opponent, to recover from a lost 14-point lead and to defend a one-score advantage for a quarter of a game against a side that was unbeaten in the competition is exactly what separates a team that competes from one that does not. That is more than a one-off.

What comes next. Wales host Australia at the Principality Stadium on Saturday 21 November 2026, kick-off 8:10pm. Our Wales v Australia head-to-head has the record, and our autumn fixtures page has the rest. New Zealand play England at Twickenham the same day, with a 2:10pm kick-off.

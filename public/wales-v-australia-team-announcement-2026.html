Wales Team to Face Australia: Four Changes as Adams and Wainwright Return

✎ By John James | 📅 19 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Wales have named their matchday 23 for Saturday's Nations Championship game against Australia, with hooker Dewi Lake captaining the side. Head coach Steve Tandy has made four changes to the starting XV from the team that beat New Zealand 35-28 last Saturday. The match is at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff and kicks off at 8:10pm on Saturday 21 November, live on ITV.

The four changes

Compared with the side that beat New Zealand, these are the four changes to the starting XV:

  • Josh Adams returns on the left wing, in place of Gabriel Hamer-Webb, who is not in the matchday 23.
  • Aaron Wainwright is back from injury at number eight, in place of Taine Basham. Wainwright missed the New Zealand game with a lower leg injury suffered in the win over Japan.
  • Alex Mann starts at number six, in place of James Botham. Botham was named player of the match against New Zealand after a 30-tackle display, but is expecting the birth of his first child this week.
  • Ben Carter starts at number five, in place of Adam Beard, who picked up a shoulder injury against New Zealand.

The result is a side that matches the starting XV that started against Japan.

Bench news

Exeter back-rower Kane James is among the replacements, and Joe Hawkins is also named. Taine Plumtree, who made an impact off the bench against New Zealand, keeps his place there. Ben Carter moves up from the bench to the starting XV, and Dan Edwards, a replacement against New Zealand, is not in the 23.

Australia team to face Wales

Wallabies coach Les Kiss has kept his backline unchanged but rebuilt the pack. Tom Hooper starts in the second row and Carlo Tizzano returns at number seven, while Isaac Kailea, Brandon Paenga-Amosa and Taniela Tupou form a new front row. Only Josh Canham, Rob Valetini and captain Harry Wilson stay in the pack from the side beaten by Scotland.

What is at stake

Wales have beaten Japan and New Zealand this November, but they still sit fifth of the six teams in the Northern Hemisphere table. This is the last round of the group stage, and the final standings decide who each side meets at Finals Weekend at Twickenham.

Australia have lost four Tests in a row, most recently 15-12 to Scotland at Murrayfield, and are fourth in the Southern Hemisphere table behind New Zealand, South Africa and Argentina. A win would give them a chance of overtaking Argentina for third, but only if Argentina lose to France.

The record against Australia

Wales and Australia have met 49 times, with Australia winning 34, Wales 14 and one game drawn. The most recent meeting was a 52-20 Australia win in Cardiff in November 2024, and Saturday is the 50th Test between the nations. For the full history, read our Wales v Australia head-to-head.

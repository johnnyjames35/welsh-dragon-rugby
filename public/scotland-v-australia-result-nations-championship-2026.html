Scotland 15-12 Australia: Russell’s Late Penalty Settles a Nervy Murrayfield Arm-Wrestle

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 15 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Scotland beat Australia 15-12 at a cold and nervy Murrayfield on Sunday 15 November, in Round 5 of the Nations Championship. Two first-half tries from Matt Fagerson and Jack Dempsey gave the hosts the edge, and Finn Russell’s late penalty made it safe, although Max Jorgensen’s try in the last minute gave the Scots a scare. Scotland missed out on a bonus point, and it was the Wallabies’ fourth defeat in a row. Rory Darge was Man of the Match and captained the side after Sione Tuipulotu pulled out before kick-off with a knee injury.

Who scored

TeamScorers
Scotland (15)Tries: Matt Fagerson, Jack Dempsey. Conversion: Finn Russell. Penalty: Russell
Australia (12)Tries: Harry Potter, Max Jorgensen. Conversion: Ben Donaldson

Half-time: Scotland 12-5 Australia. The reports we checked differ by a minute on some scores, so we have kept the timings loose.

First half: Scotland strike twice

Scotland made a fast start. They drove up to the Wallabies line and spun the ball wide to an unmarked Fagerson for a try in the opening ten minutes, although Russell hit the post with the conversion. Australia replied within a few minutes. Harry Potter finished a counter-attack on the left, getting past a weak tackle from Russell after some neat handling in the build-up.

Scotland went back in front just before the half-hour through old-fashioned forward power. Jack Dempsey, who is Sydney-born and won 14 caps for the Wallabies before switching allegiance in 2022, burrowed over from close range after Australia had stopped a first maul. Russell converted, and it was 12-5 at the break.

Second half: a long wait for points

It was a highly physical, error-filled second half, and no points were scored for more than 40 minutes. Australia were then penalised at a ruck in the 75th minute, and Scotland chose to kick for goal rather than chase a bonus-point try. Russell landed the penalty from mid-range to make it 15-5.

Australia had one more say. Jorgensen scampered down the right wing in the last minute for a try that Donaldson converted, and the Wallabies got one last chance after the restart, again down Jorgensen’s flank, but time ran out. Scotland lifted the Hopetoun Cup for what was their fifth win in six meetings with Australia.

Scotland head coach Gregor Townsend said afterwards that it was one of the best defensive performances he had seen from his side in a couple of years.

What it means

The win moves Scotland above Wales into fourth place in the northern hemisphere table, though without the bonus point they wanted. They host Japan next Saturday, 21 November. It is a bad result for Australia, who had conceded seven tries in each of their previous three games and have now lost four in a row.

The Wallabies travel to Cardiff next to face Wales on Saturday 21 November. Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 on Saturday for a first win over the All Blacks in 73 years: see our Wales v New Zealand match report and the Wales v Australia head-to-head.

Wales v Japan at Twickenham: Nations Championship Ninth-Place Play-off Preview

✎ By John James | 📅 25 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read
Kick-off: 1:10pm UK time, Saturday 28 November 2026 at the Allianz Stadium, Twickenham. Live on ITV1 and ITVX, with S4C for Welsh-language coverage. Free to air.

Wales finish the inaugural Nations Championship at Twickenham on Saturday, against a side they beat three weeks ago. The match is the ninth-place play-off, part of a Finals Weekend where all 12 teams play at the same ground between Friday 27 and Sunday 29 November. Here is what is at stake, how the two sides got here and when to watch.

What is the ninth-place play-off?

The Nations Championship ends with a Finals Weekend at Twickenham, which now goes by the name Allianz Stadium. Every team plays one more match, and each pairs sides of the same rank from the two pools. Wales and Japan are both fifth in their pool, so they meet for ninth place overall. There is no trophy at stake, only a final placing in the first edition of the competition.

How Wales got here

Wales began their autumn at home to Japan and have not played them since. Their three November matches in Cardiff went like this:

  • 7 November: Wales 28-21 Japan.
  • 14 November: Wales 35-28 New Zealand, a first win over the All Blacks since 1953. See our Wales v New Zealand guide.
  • 21 November: Wales 26-29 Australia. Read the match report.

Steve Tandy is head coach. The Japan win was his fourth as Wales boss.

The last meeting: Wales 28-21 Japan

Wales opened their Nations Championship autumn with a nervy bonus-point win at the Principality Stadium. Louis Rees-Zammit was man of the match and finished a well-worked team try. Josh Adams scored two tries, the second after the break securing the bonus point, and Sam Costelow converted all three first-half scores to give Wales a 21-14 half-time lead. Dewi Lake captained Wales.

Japan scored through Ben Gunter and captain Warner Dearns in the first half, and Kenta Fukuda's converted try five minutes from time set up a tense finish that the Welsh defence held. Wales led by seven at the end, which is a sign that Japan will not make life easy at Twickenham.

Team news

At the time of writing, neither side's team for Saturday had been confirmed in the sources we checked. We will add the line-ups once they are announced. Our Wales v Japan head-to-head record has the full history between the two.

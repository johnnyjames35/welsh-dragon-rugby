Ospreys 31-14 Dragons: Storm-Soaked Win Christens the Rebuilt St Helen's

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 25 October 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

The Ospreys are home. On a night when the weather did its very worst, they marked their return to St Helen’s, the seafront ground the region left in 2005, with a commanding 31-14 bonus-point win over the Dragons in front of 5,592 supporters.

I was in the ground, and it felt like a rugby match from the old days. The weather was atrocious, yet the supporters stayed loud and the atmosphere was brilliant from start to finish.

A fast start in the wind and rain

The Ospreys had been on the road for the whole of the new season, with trips to Durban, Johannesburg, Tbilisi and Newport while the ground was finished. After a tough start in South Africa, this was their third win in a row.

With the wind at their backs, they struck first. Scrum-half Reuben Morgan-Williams burrowed over from close range after six minutes, with Dan Edwards adding the extras. The Dragons played well into the wind and were the better side for a spell, but they were punished for failing to fall on the ball after a grubber kick, and Morgan-Williams pounced for his second on the half hour.

Just before the break, Edwards found a brave penalty touch-finder and the pack drove over from a five-metre line-out for captain Sam Parry to score. The Dragons had the last word of the half, though, as recalled Wales wing Rio Dyer crossed in the corner with the final play to make it 19-7.

The line-out drive does the damage

The Ospreys’ maul decided the game. Straight after the restart they were awarded a penalty try when a 20-metre line-out drive was stopped illegally, and Dragons scrum-half Rhodri Williams was sent to the sin bin.

The visitors then lost Ben Carter to a head injury, and Dyer did not reappear after half-time. When Tinus de Beer followed Williams to the bin, Edwards kicked to the corner and the Ospreys pack did the rest, with back-rower Ross Moriarty scoring against his former club.

Anzelo Tuitavuki gave the Dragons a flicker of hope with a try around 15 minutes from the end, but Aneurin Owen’s yellow card for a head-on-head tackle on Iestyn Hopkin ended any thoughts of a comeback.

What the head coach said

Ospreys head coach Mark Jones called it “a proper Osprey performance” and praised the way his players handled the pressure of a first night back. Two of their five tries came from the maul and a third was the penalty try from a line-out drive.

A night to remember at the ground

The ground still has some rough edges and a long list of jobs to finish, but there was no shortage of atmosphere. Season ticket sales have more than doubled, and St Helen’s will also be home to Swansea RFC and community clubs. One supporter even beat the conditions at half-time to win a new £20,000 bathroom by passing a ball through a hole.

What next?

The Ospreys had beaten the Dragons 38-31 at Rodney Parade a fortnight earlier, so they have taken maximum points from both derbies this season. The Dragons, who also have a few worries ahead of Wales’ Nations Championship campaign, return to Newport to face Zebre.

World Rugby Rankings: Where Wales Stand and How the Table Works

✎ By John James | 📅 22 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Wales climbed to ninth in the World Rugby men’s rankings after beating New Zealand for the first time in 73 years. This page shows the current top ten, explains how the points system works and puts Wales’ position in context. It is updated by hand after each official update from World Rugby.

Latest table: 17 November 2026, after Round 5 of the Nations Championship. Wales lost 29-26 to Australia in Round 6 on 21 November, and World Rugby normally publishes updated rankings at midday on the Monday after a weekend of internationals. How the rankings work

World Rugby’s system is a points exchange. Every team has a rating, and when two teams play, points move from one to the other, so whatever one side gains the other loses. The main rules are:

  • Home advantage: the home side is treated as three rating points stronger than it really is, so a home win earns less and a home defeat costs more.
  • The gap matters: beating a higher-rated team earns more points than beating a lower-rated one, which is why the win over New Zealand moved Wales so far.
  • Big wins count extra: a win by more than 15 points multiplies the exchange by 1.5. A narrow win or defeat is not adjusted.
  • Rugby World Cup matches count double.
  • Only full internationals count, between full member unions. Apart from the World Cup, every qualifying match is weighted equally, including friendlies.

A team’s rating only changes when it plays, but its position can still move when other teams play. A team can drop a place without taking the field if a rival below it wins by enough to overtake it. When the gap between two sides is very large, the stronger side can win and gain no points at all.

Quick answers

Where are Wales in the World Rugby rankings?

Wales were ninth in the World Rugby men's rankings on 17 November 2026, up one place after beating New Zealand 35-28. Rankings are updated after each round of internationals, so this can change after Round 6.

How do the World Rugby rankings work?

Teams swap rating points after every full international. The size of the swap depends on the result and the gap between the teams. The home side is treated as three points stronger, a win by more than 15 points counts 1.5 times, and Rugby World Cup matches count double.

What is Wales' lowest ever World Rugby ranking?

Wales' lowest ever ranking is 14th, reached after a defeat to Japan on 5 July 2025. Wales had never been lower than 14th since World Rugby introduced the men's rankings in 2003.

Have Wales ever been number one in the world rankings?

Yes. Wales briefly topped the rankings in 2019, during a three-month period when New Zealand, Wales, Ireland, England and South Africa each held first place.

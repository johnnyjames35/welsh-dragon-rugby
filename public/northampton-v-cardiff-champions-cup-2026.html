Northampton 28-12 Cardiff: Rain, a Second-Half Fightback and No Upset at Franklin's Gardens

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 18 October 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Cardiff began their 2026-27 Champions Cup campaign with a 28-12 defeat away to Northampton Saints on Sunday evening. Torrential rain made handling a lottery, Saints scored twice in the first half, and Cardiff came back to within two points before the English champions pulled clear again.

Cardiff could not take a point from the trip, but they pushed a side who had scored 11 tries in their previous game right to the wire for long spells.

How the game turned

Saints strike twice on the break

Cardiff had the better of the early territory, but the wet ball and a stubborn Saints defence meant they could not turn it into points. Northampton scored from their first real chance. On 12 minutes, wing George Hendy broke the line and the move ended with full-back James Pater scoring. Anthony Belleau converted.

The second try came on about 20 minutes. Grady took the ball back over his own try line, and Saints were awarded a scrum free-kick. The ball was moved wide and centre Toby Thame went over, with Belleau adding the extras for 14-0.

A try that was not

Cardiff thought they had a reply before the break. Lock Tom Manz looked to have scored after a strong run from Grady, but the TMO ruled that he had lost control of the ball over the line, and the on-field decision was overturned. Manz also had to go off for a head injury assessment. A shaky line-out then cost Cardiff another chance in the Saints 22, and they went in 14 points behind.

Cardiff come back

Cardiff stayed on the front foot at the start of the second half. Seven minutes after the restart, centre Harri Millard crashed over from a tapped penalty close to the line, and Jarrod Evans converted to make it 14-7.

Then came the moment that made the game. Belleau was sent to the sin bin for hands in the ruck with Saints defending their own 22, and Cardiff tapped quickly and worked the ball right, where Mason Grady dived over in the corner. Evans' touchline conversion fell short under pressure from Ollie Sleightholme, who raced out to try to charge it down, and it stayed 14-12.

Hendy and Dingwall close it out

Saints responded while a man down. After a penalty took Saints to the corner, Hendy juggled the ball and got it down for his side's third try, and Pater converted for 21-12 with 15 minutes left. Hendy then raced back to stop Josh Adams from scoring in the corner at the other end.

Late on, substitute Fraser Dingwall ran in from the edge of the Cardiff 22, and Belleau converted to make it 28-12.

Why there was no bonus point

Saints finished with four tries to Cardiff's two. Under the new bonus-point rules that was not enough for a try bonus, so Northampton left without the bonus point.

Bonus points have changed. A team no longer gets a try bonus simply for scoring four tries. Our Dragons v Perpignan report explains how the new rule works in the Challenge Cup.

Cardiff's team

Cardiff made nine changes for the trip, and scrum-half Aled Davies was then ruled out before kick-off through illness, with Johan Mulder starting instead. Daf Hughes captained a side that included Taulupe Faletau at number eight, and it was a mix of experience and youth, with a number of Wales internationals given the weekend off.

Callum Sheedy replaced Evans on 62 minutes, and Josh Adams came on at centre earlier in the second half.

What comes next for Cardiff

There is little time to dwell on it. Cardiff face a five-day turnaround before the URC resumes against the Hollywoodbets Sharks. In Europe, they are in Pool 4 with Northampton, Bath, Montpellier, Stade Français Paris and the Bulls.

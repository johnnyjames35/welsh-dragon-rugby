Wales' Autumn 2026 Fixtures: Japan, New Zealand and Australia at the Principality Stadium

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 1 October 2026 | ⏱ 5 min read

Wales' autumn is now part of something bigger than a run of friendlies. The November window is the second half of the inaugural Nations Championship, and Steve Tandy's side have three home matches on three consecutive Saturdays at the Principality Stadium: Japan, New Zealand and Australia.

Here is everything Welsh fans need: the dates, kick-off times and TV details, where Wales stand after the July tour, and what is actually at stake before Finals Weekend at Twickenham.

Wales' November fixtures

RoundDateMatchKick-off
Round 4Sat 7 NovemberWales v Japan4:40pm
Round 5Sat 14 NovemberWales v New Zealand2:10pm
Round 6Sat 21 NovemberWales v Australia8:10pm

All three matches are at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, and the WRU has confirmed they are live on ITV. Kick-off times are UK time. It is worth noting the Australia game is an evening match, which will feel very different from the two afternoon fixtures.

Where Wales stand after July

The July tour was a mixed bag, and the table reflects it. Wales opened with a win over Fiji in Cardiff, then ran into two tough southern hemisphere sides.

One win from three leaves Wales fifth in the Northern Hemisphere table with five points, ahead of only Italy. France lead on 12 points, with Scotland on 11 and England and Ireland both on 10.

That makes November the chance to change the story. Wales are five points behind fourth-placed Ireland, and a couple of wins with bonus points would close that gap, although every side above them still has three matches to play in this window too.

What each opponent brings

Japan: Saturday 7 November

Japan are the opening test, and on paper the match Wales will most fancy winning. Japan lost 42-15 to France on 18 July, so they will not arrive in Cardiff unbeaten. Wales need results to climb the table, and starting the window with a win would set the tone for the two heavyweight games that follow.

New Zealand: Saturday 14 November

The All Blacks came through the July window unbeaten. They finished level on 15 points with South Africa at the top of the Southern Hemisphere table and were separated only on points difference. This is the biggest occasion of the autumn in Cardiff, and the biggest ask.

Australia: Saturday 21 November

The Wallabies lost their first two matches of the July window, so their November form will be worth watching. With an evening kick-off in Cardiff, it has the makings of a proper occasion to end the home campaign.

What is at stake: Finals Weekend

The Nations Championship ends with Finals Weekend at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham, from Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November. Six matches are played across the three days. Teams are paired with the side finishing in the same position in the other hemisphere's table, so fifth in the north plays fifth in the south, and the two top-ranked sides meet in the final.

A win is worth four points, with bonus points on offer, and the six matches in the two windows decide where each team finishes. So there is no such thing as a throwaway match this autumn: where Wales finish in the Northern table decides who they play at Twickenham.

No relegation worries. The first two editions of the Nations Championship, in 2026 and 2027, do not include relegation. That takes some pressure off Wales' rebuild, but it does not take away the pride, or the reputation that comes with finishing higher.

Tickets and how to watch

The WRU has said adult tickets for the three Wales matches at the Principality Stadium start from £26, and it has introduced concession tickets for under-18s and students. It is also running a "Reward your roots" scheme that lets supporters give back to their local grassroots club when they buy. Prices and availability change, so always check the official WRU ticket site rather than third-party sellers.

If you cannot get to Cardiff, all three matches are live on ITV.

The bigger picture for Wales

This is a team still rebuilding. Wales finished bottom of the 2026 Six Nations, although they ended a long losing run with a 31-17 win over Italy in Cardiff, and the July tour brought one win from three. Nobody is expecting a sudden transformation. But a home win over Japan, a competitive performance against the All Blacks and a proper fight against Australia would be real evidence of progress, and that is what Welsh fans will be looking for.

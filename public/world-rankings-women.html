Women's World Rugby Rankings: Where Wales Women Stand

✎ By John James | 📅 23 November 2026 | ⏱ 4 min read

Wales Women are 11th in the World Rugby women’s rankings, just outside the top ten. This page shows the table down to twelfth, explains how the points system works and puts Wales Women’s position in context. It is updated by hand when World Rugby’s women’s table changes. How the rankings work

World Rugby publishes one explanation for both its men’s and women’s rankings. The system is a points exchange: every team has a rating, and when two teams play, points move from one to the other, so whatever one side gains the other loses. The main rules are:

  • Home advantage: the home side is treated as three rating points stronger than it really is, so a home win earns less and a home defeat costs more.
  • The gap matters: beating a higher-rated team earns more points than beating a lower-rated one.
  • Big wins count extra: a win by more than 15 points multiplies the exchange by 1.5.
  • Women’s Rugby World Cup matches count double.

A team’s rating only changes when it plays, but its position can still move when other teams play. Women’s internationals are less frequent than men’s, so the table can stay the same for weeks at a time.

Quick answers

Where are Wales Women in the World Rugby rankings?

Wales Women were 11th in the World Rugby women’s rankings on 23 November 2026, on 70.12 points. They are 0.64 points behind tenth-placed South Africa and 3.47 points ahead of Fiji in twelfth. Rankings change only when women’s internationals are played.

What is Wales Women’s lowest ever World Rugby ranking?

RugbyPass reported 13th as the lowest ever position for Wales Women, reached in 2026 after Fiji beat Japan. Wales were 11th on 23 November 2026, two places higher.

How do the World Rugby women’s rankings work?

Teams swap rating points after every full international. The size of the swap depends on the result and the gap between the teams. The home side is treated as three points stronger, a win by more than 15 points counts 1.5 times, and Women’s Rugby World Cup matches count double.

From Nine Defeats to Three Wins From Four: How Wales Women Turned 2026 Around

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 7 November 2026 | ⏱ 5 min read

At the start of the autumn, Wales Women had lost nine Tests in a row. They had finished bottom of the 2026 Women's Six Nations without a win and gone out of the 2025 World Cup at the pool stage. Two months later, they have won three of their four WXV Global Series games, including their first back-to-back Test wins since June 2024. Here is how Sean Lynn's side turned their year around.

The four results

Across the four games Wales scored 120 points and conceded 50.

Where it started: a long, hard run

The 2026 Six Nations was a campaign to forget: five matches, five defeats, ending with a 43-24 home loss to Italy. Wales had won just one Test in 2025. By September, the losing run had reached nine Tests.

Lynn has said the staff held a big meeting in July to reset how the team works. The autumn was the first chance to see whether it had made a difference.

The night it clicked: Wales 50-19 South Africa

The answer came at Cardiff Arms Park on 18 September. Wales scored eight tries, led 38-5 at half-time and claimed their first Test win in 14 months. Prop Sisilia Tuipulotu scored twice, and Lynn's decision to start Keira Bevan at fly-half for the first time paid off.

The near miss: Wales 24-26 USA

A week later, Wales lost to the USA, but the manner of it mattered. They trailed 21-5, fought back to lead 24-21 at half-time, and were only beaten by a hat-trick from USA number eight Freda Tafuna. For a team that had grown used to heavy defeats, a two-point loss to a side ranked above them was a sign of real belief.

Two wins in Japan

In Tokyo on 24 October, Wales ground out a 17-0 win in wind and rain. Scrum-half Seren Lockwood scored twice, hooker Kelsey Jones added a third try, and co-captain Alex Callender won her 50th cap. The one blow was a knee injury to centre Freya Bell, one of the stand-out players of the autumn.

A week later in Kumagaya, Wales won 29-5 with five tries: two from wing Amy Williams, and one each from Nel Metcalfe, Kelsey Jones and Sisilia Tuipulotu. They finished the game with 13 players after yellow cards for Keira Bevan and Hannah Dallavalle, and held on. Callender's turnover on her own line stopped Japan scoring a second try.

What changed

  • Defence. Wales conceded five points in two Tests in Japan, and kept a clean sheet in Tokyo.
  • Belief. Coming back from 16 points down against the USA, and holding out with 13 players in Kumagaya, are not things a team on a nine-game losing run usually does.
  • New options. Keira Bevan at fly-half, and debuts for Shanelle Williams and Elan Jones against South Africa, gave Lynn more choices.
  • Tries from everywhere. Props, hookers, scrum-halves, wings and back-rowers all scored this autumn.

A note of realism

This is not the finished article. Wales and Japan are ranked close together, around 11th and 12th in the world, and the USA showed that the higher-ranked sides are still a step above. The real test comes in the Six Nations, where Wales open at home to England, who have won eight Six Nations titles in a row. But for the first time in a long while, Wales go into a new year with wins behind them.

Quick answers

How did Wales Women do in the 2026 WXV Global Series?

Wales won three of their four matches: 50-19 against South Africa, 17-0 and 29-5 against Japan. Their only defeat was 26-24 against the USA. They scored 120 points and conceded 50.

Is this Wales Women's first time winning two Tests in a row?

No. The two wins in Japan were Wales' first back-to-back Test victories since June 2024.

How long was Wales Women's losing run?

Wales had lost nine Tests in a row before beating South Africa 50-19 in September 2026. That win was their first Test victory in 14 months.

When do Wales Women play next?

Wales open the 2027 Women's Six Nations at home to England on Saturday 10 April 2027, kicking off at 2:45pm.

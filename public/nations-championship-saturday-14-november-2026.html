Nations Championship Saturday: Kick-Off Times, TV and Team News for All Four Games

Welsh Dragon Rugby | 📅 14 November 2026 | ⏱ 5 min read

Four Nations Championship games are being played today, starting before lunch in Genoa and ending under the lights in Dublin. The one that matters most to us is Wales v New Zealand at the Principality Stadium, where Steve Tandy has made four changes and the All Blacks have made ten. Here is the running order, where to watch and the confirmed team news for each match.

Italy v Argentina is listed as a 12:40 kick-off in Italy, which is 11:40 in the UK. Teams can still change up to an hour before kick-off, so treat the line-ups below as correct at the time of writing.

Wales v New Zealand (14:10)

Wales beat Japan 28-21 last Saturday. New Zealand arrive after beating Scotland 43-21 at Murrayfield and are unbeaten in the tournament. Wales have not beaten the All Blacks since 1953, and today is the 39th Test between the two sides. The referee is France’s Pierre Brousset.

Wales team news

  • Four changes from the side that beat Japan: Adam Beard returns at lock, Gabriel Hamer-Webb replaces Josh Adams on the left wing, and in the back row James Botham starts at blindside with Taine Basham at No. 8.
  • Basham’s return is his first Wales appearance since the 2024 Six Nations. Aaron Wainwright is out with a minor calf injury.
  • Rhys Carre is not in the match-day squad. Tandy has said it is a selection decision, not an injury. Nicky Smith starts at loosehead, with Gareth Thomas on the bench.
  • Tomas Francis has been released from the squad with a knee injury, and Keiron Assiratti has been called up in his place.
  • Dewi Lake captains the side. Ryan Elias and Taine Plumtree are among the replacements.

New Zealand team news

  • Beauden Barrett captains the All Blacks for the first time, in his 149th Test, and starts at full-back.
  • Richie Mo’unga returns at fly-half after the calf injury that kept him out of the Bledisloe Cup Tests.
  • Ten changes in all from the team that played Scotland. Anton Segner and Fehi Fineanganofo both make their first Test starts, and Damian McKenzie and Cam Roigard are rested.
  • Jordie Barrett and Will Jordan are unavailable this week.

For more on the game, see our Wales v New Zealand head-to-head record, the Wales team announcement and how to watch Wales v New Zealand.

England v Japan (16:40)

Steve Borthwick has made ten changes to the side that beat Australia 45-22 last Sunday. Maro Itoje captains, with Ellis Genge and George Ford as vice-captains.

England XV: 15 Freddie Steward, 14 Will Joseph, 13 Henry Slade, 12 Max Ojomoh, 11 Cadan Murley, 10 George Ford, 9 Jack van Poortvliet, 8 Henry Pollock, 7 Ben Earl, 6 Alex Coles, 5 George Martin, 4 Maro Itoje (c), 3 Asher Opoku-Fordjour, 2 Luke Cowan-Dickie, 1 Ellis Genge.

  • Pollock starts at No. 8 after two tries off the bench against Australia, and Earl moves to openside.
  • Immanuel Feyi-Waboso (ankle) and George Furbank (groin) are injured, which is why Joseph, Murley and Steward form the back three.
  • Scrum-half Archie McParland is on the bench and could make his Test debut.

England host New Zealand next Saturday. Read our England v Australia match report for how they got here.

Ireland v Fiji (20:10)

Andy Farrell has made seven changes from the side that beat Argentina last week. Tadhg Beirne captains, because Dan Sheehan is unavailable after a concussion against Argentina.

Ireland XV: 15 Mack Hansen, 14 Robert Baloucoune, 13 Jamie Osborne, 12 Bundee Aki, 11 Tommy O’Brien, 10 Sam Prendergast, 9 Jamison Gibson-Park, 1 Andrew Porter, 2 Ronan Kelleher, 3 Tadhg Furlong, 4 J Ryan, 5 Tadhg Beirne (c), 6 R Baird, 7 Josh van der Flier, 8 N Timoney.

  • Hansen moves to full-back, with Baloucoune and O’Brien on the wings.
  • Ulster’s James McNabney is on the bench and could make his Ireland debut.
  • Fiji coach Jake White has kept only six of the starters from their heavy defeat to France in Lyon.
  • Ireland face South Africa at the Aviva Stadium next Saturday, and Farrell has said how his players perform against Fiji will influence that selection.

Our Ireland v Argentina report covers last week’s game.

Italy v Argentina (11:40)

Gonzalo Quesada has made five changes after Italy’s 66-7 defeat to South Africa in Turin, going with his most experienced XV. Argentina, who lost to Ireland in Dublin last week, are captained by Pablo Matera.

Italy XV: 15 Tommaso Allan, 14 Ange Capuozzo, 13 Paolo Odogwu, 12 Leonardo Marin, 11 Monty Ioane, 10 Paolo Garbisi, 9 Stephen Varney, 8 Lorenzo Cannone, 7 Manuel Zuliani, 6 Ross Vintcent, 5 Federico Ruzza, 4 Niccolò Cannone, 3 Simone Ferrari, 2 Giacomo Nicotera (c), 1 Danilo Fischetti.

Argentina XV: 15 Bautista Delguy, 14 Rodrigo Isgró, 13 Justo Piccardo, 12 Santiago Chocobares, 11 Mateo Carreras, 10 Tomás Albornoz, 9 Simón Benítez Cruz, 8 Juan Martín Scelzo, 7 Joaquín Moro, 6 Pablo Matera (c), 5 Pedro Rubiolo, 4 Guido Petti, 3 Francisco Moreno, 2 Bautista Bernasconi, 1 Boris Wenger.

Heavy rain fell in Genoa on Friday, and Quesada has said he expects a scrappy game. It is also a Top 14 meeting in the half-backs, with Garbisi and Albornoz facing each other.

What happens next

The Nations Championship Finals Weekend is at Twickenham from 27 to 29 November. Wales play Australia in Cardiff on Saturday 21 November. We will publish match reports after full time, starting with Wales v New Zealand.

Quick answers

What time are the Nations Championship games on Saturday 14 November 2026?

Italy v Argentina kicks off at 11:40 UK time (12:40 in Genoa), Wales v New Zealand at 14:10, England v Japan at 16:40 and Ireland v Fiji at 20:10.

What TV channel is Wales v New Zealand on?

Wales v New Zealand at the Principality Stadium (kick-off 14:10) is live on ITV and S4C.

Who captains Wales and New Zealand on Saturday?

Dewi Lake captains Wales from hooker. Beauden Barrett captains the All Blacks for the first time, in his 149th Test.

Who is in the Wales team to face New Zealand?

Wales: Blair Murray, Louis Rees-Zammit, Eddie James, Max Llewellyn, Gabriel Hamer-Webb, Sam Costelow, Tomos Williams, Taine Basham, Jac Morgan, James Botham, Adam Beard, Dafydd Jenkins, Archie Griffin, Dewi Lake (captain) and Nicky Smith.
